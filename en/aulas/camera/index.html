Lesson 3 · 16 min

The camera as a verb: each movement tells something

Camera movement weighs heavily on the result of a generated video, and a common mistake is stacking movements in the same shot. In this lesson each movement gets a function: move closer, pull back, follow, reveal, orbit, or shake. One per shot, chosen for its effect on the audience.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Match six intentions to the movements that deliver them
  2. 2Tell camera movement apart from lens movement
  3. 3Use one movement and one style per shot
  4. 4Vary the movement between neighboring shots

Goal: choose one movement per shot by intent and write, for each shot, the effect it causes in whoever is watching.

02 · The rules of movement

The example continues in the bakery.

1. One movement per shot

Orbit plus push in plus pan plus zoom: the camera fights itself and the shot comes out unstable. One movement gives the generator a single direction to carry out.

If the scene calls for two movements, it calls for two shots.

Weak × directedWeak: an orbit with a zoom and a dolly on the oven. Directed: a slow push in on the oven while the door opens.

2. Camera or lens?

In a dolly the camera moves: foreground and background shift at different rates and the space feels real. In a zoom only the lens changes: the image gets closer without the camera leaving its spot, flatter and more observational.

The dolly zoom does both at the same time, in opposite directions: the background seems to stretch. It's disorienting; use it once, at the right moment.

3. One style per shot

Style is the texture of the movement. Steadicam is smooth and controlled; handheld is raw and urgent; slow motion stretches time and gives weight.

Two styles in the same shot dilute both intentions. Pick the dominant feeling and stick with it.

4. Vary between neighbors

Two neighboring shots with the same movement kill the rhythm. Match the movement to the job of the moment: opening calls for a side tracking shot or a still camera; tension calls for a slow move in; a reveal calls for an orbit or a tilt.

To close, use a final move in, or a pull back that gives the place back. End on a strong image.

fluidity, following without cutting → travelling → tracking shot, truck left/right

revealing vertically → tilt → tilt up/down
