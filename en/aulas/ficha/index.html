Lesson 6 · 17 min

Character, wardrobe, and setting: the sheet that holds consistency

One of the most visible mistakes in generated video is the character who changes face, clothes, or hair from one shot to the next. The defense is a written character sheet and a pack of reference images, decided before the first generation and repeated in every shot.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Describe identity, wardrobe, and prop in detail that repeats
  2. 2Write the lock: what never changes between shots
  3. 3Build the reference image pack
  4. 4Separate look from composition

Goal: write the sheet for the character and setting you'll reuse as a reference in every shot, without imitating a real person.

02 · How to build the character sheet

The character in the first batch is fictional: Dona Lia, 58 years old.

1. A detail that repeats

The generator doesn't remember the previous shot. What holds the character together is you repeating the same words in every prompt.

That's why the character sheet uses concrete detail: color, material, shape, a mark on the face. "Friendly" changes from shot to shot; "freckles on the nose and thin-rimmed glasses" doesn't.

Dona Lia's wardrobeNavy canvas apron over a white shirt with rolled-up sleeves, gray scarf over the hair.

2. Reference through several images

The 2026 generators accept several reference images to keep character, prop and style consistent. Dated examples (Oct/2026): Veo 3.1 accepts up to 3 images; Seedance 2.5, up to 30 images, 10 videos and 10 audios.

Kling 3.0's "elements" are reported by third-party sources, still without official confirmation. The names change; the character sheet and the image pack work for all of them.

3. Appearance on one side, composition on the other

The character's reference image says what they look like. The framing, angle, and pose come from your shot. Mixing the two can lead the generator to copy the reference's pose instead of following your direction.

That's why the references are made in a neutral studio, neutral pose, natural light and plain background: appearance only, no scene.

4. Don't imitate a real person

A fictional character can't look like a recognizable real person, nor use a real brand. An avatar with your own face only with your consent.

Labeling, image and voice rights, and model licensing are covered in course 2, lesson 7.

03 · Templates to copy

The character sheet and the request for the reference images in a neutral studio.

ficha.txt (template)
Name: <name of the fictional character>
Age: <apparent age>
Face: <shape, marks, eyes, resting expression>
Hair: <color, length, how it's tied up>
Body: <height, build, posture>
Wardrobe: <pieces, colors and materials>
Prop: <the scene prop that carries the story>
Setting: <the place, the materials, the time>
Light: <the light from the look card, in one line>
Never changes: <the list of what stays the same in every shot>
Character reference request (image generator)
Character reference photograph, neutral studio, plain gray background, soft natural light, neutral pose, full body from the front, looking at the camera. <paste here the Age, Face, Hair, Body and Wardrobe from the character sheet>. Fictional character, no resemblance to a real person, no text, no logo.
