Lesson 2 · 16 min

Shot size, angle and distance: the grammar every generator understands

Shot size and angle aren't aesthetics: they decide what the audience sees and how they feel about the character. And it's a vocabulary that Google's official video prompt guide lists with names and examples. You leave with your scene's shot ladder written in the terms the prompt uses.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Read the shot ladder, from the wide shot to the detail
  2. 2Use angle to convey power, fragility or unease
  3. 3Know what the lens does to space
  4. 4Write each choice with the term the generator understands

Goal: choose the shot size and angle based on the audience's emotion, and write the scene's shot ladder in the generator's terms.

01 · The shot ladder

From the widest to the tightest. Each step answers a question.

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

Decision rule: if the place or the action matters more, go wide; if the emotion matters more, go tight; if a detail changes the story, go to the detail.

02 · Size, angle and lens

Three decisions per shot, always based on what the audience should feel.

1. Size is emotion

A wide shot answers "where are we?". A close shot answers "how does this feel?". The detail shows what changes the story.

A path that works: open wide to show the world, move in to reveal the emotion, and end on a close-up or detail for impact.

In the batchWide shot of the dark street with the bakery lit up; medium shot of Dona Lia at the counter; close-up of her face lit by the oven.

2. Angle is a power relationship

Eye level is neutral. A high angle, looking down, makes the character small or vulnerable. A low angle, looking up, makes them powerful or imposing.

A top-down shot gives a map-like view; a worm's-eye view exaggerates height; a dutch angle (tilted camera) brings discomfort. Use the dutch angle rarely, or it loses its force.

3. The warning from the official guide

Google's video prompt guide (Veo and Gemini Omni Flash, page updated on 10/15/2026) lists these angles and warns: some advanced angles and lenses are not officially supported, and results vary.

In practice, a simple, well-justified angle comes out more stable than a rare one. If the rare one doesn't work, swap it for a simple one with the same intent.

4. The lens changes the space

A wide-angle lens opens the field and exaggerates perspective: the place looks bigger. A telephoto lens narrows the field and compresses space: the background looks glued on and the subject is isolated.

Shallow depth of field keeps only the subject sharp and blurs the rest. It is a quiet way to say where to look (lesson 5).

Swap · the most important shot with another angle

5 min

Pick the shot that carries the emotion of the scene and imagine it from another angle. At the end of the escada.txt, write a line starting with Swap: with the new angle and what changes in the audience's feeling.

Done when: the escada.txt has, after the shots, a line that starts with Troca: with an angle different from the original and the effect in one sentence.
