Lesson 5 · 15 min

Composition and depth in the frame

A beautiful frame where the eye doesn't know where to go communicates nothing. Composition is deciding what you see first, where empty space works in your favor, and how foreground, midground and background separate. It's what makes a generated image feel like real space instead of a flat backdrop.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Apply the one-second test to any frame
  2. 2Choose between thirds, center and diagonal by emotion
  3. 3Use empty space to isolate or squeeze
  4. 4Set up foreground, midground and background and separate the layers

Goal: compose each frame with a subject you understand in one second and three layers of depth separated by focus, light or color.

02 · Composition in practice

The examples come from the bakery.

1. The one-second test

Look at the frame for one second and say out loud what the subject is. If you hesitate, the audience will hesitate too.

One dominant subject per frame. Product, person and a background detail fighting for attention make a beautiful image that says nothing.

2. Thirds, center or diagonal

Thirds for natural flow; center and symmetry for control and monumentality; diagonal for energy and action. The choice follows the emotion of the shot, not taste.

Vary it across the scene: three frames in a row with the same composition feel repetitive.

In the batchOven door in the center, symmetrical: the ritual. The row of loaves on a diagonal: the energy of the first batch.

3. Negative space

Before you generate, decide: should the audience feel room to breathe or a tight squeeze? The amount of emptiness answers that before any other choice.

Dona Lia small in a corner of the frame, with the dark street filling the rest, tells the loneliness of someone who wakes up before the city.

4. Three layers, kept apart

Having foreground, midground and background is not enough: they need to be distinct. Shallow depth of field blurs the foreground or the background; backlight cuts out the subject; color and haze push the background back.

With the layers separated, the sideways camera movement (lesson 3) makes each one move at its own pace, and the space feels real.

03 · Template to copy

One line per frame: the composition, the three layers and where the eye goes.

quadros.txt (template)
Scene: <scene name>
1 | <composition> | foreground: <what is close to the camera> | midground: <the subject> | background: <the context> | the eye goes to: <the subject>
2 | <composition> | foreground: <...> | midground: <...> | background: <...> | the eye goes to: <...>
# compositions: rule of thirds, center (symmetry), diagonal, leading lines, negative space, frame within a frame

One second · the out-loud test

5 min

For each frame, read only the "the eye goes to" line and check that there is just one subject. If the line names two things, take light or focus away from one of them and rewrite it.

Done when: in every line of quadros.txt, after o olho vai para:, there is a single subject.

07 · To keep

One subjectThe audience understands the frame in one second, without searching.
Composition by emotionThirds for flow, center for control, diagonal for energy, empty space to isolate.
