Lesson 4 · 16 min

Light, color and atmosphere: the look decided before the prompt

"Cinematic light" in a prompt says nothing: the generator needs to know where the light comes from, which side it hits, whether it is hard or soft, and what color it is. In this lesson you decide the light for your scene and write the look card, the fixed block that goes into every prompt for it.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Give every light a source the audience believes
  2. 2Use the direction and quality of light to shape the character
  3. 3Take light away to gain depth
  4. 4Lock palette and atmosphere into a look card

Goal: decide the source, direction, quality, temperature and atmosphere of your scene's light and write the look card that goes into every prompt.

02 · How to decide the look

During the batch, the lit-up bakery is the only light on the street.

1. Take light away before you add it

Lighting everything makes the image flat: everything visible, nothing felt. Good cinema is also deciding where the light doesn't reach.

On a set, the black flag that blocks light is called negative fill. In a prompt, ask for shadow with words: low-key, deep shadows, one side of the face in the dark.

2. Name the source

Replace "cinematic lighting" with a source and a side: "oven light coming from the right", "cold glow of a screen".

A named source adds credibility and also pins down direction and temperature at once.

In the batchSide light from the oven on the right, soft on the counter; outside, only a cold streetlamp on the dark street.

3. One palette per scene

The same dominant tones in every shot tie the scene together. Each shot with a different temperature makes the scene fall apart into pieces.

Pick three or four colors and repeat them. If the story calls for a change, change it on purpose, at a turning point.

4. The look card

The look card gathers the five decisions and the palette. It goes into every prompt of the scene exactly the same, and that holds continuity.

In course 1 it becomes the light and style block of the prompt. Here you only decide.

03 · Templates to copy

The look card and the lighting phrase in the format of the English prompts.

look.txt (template)
Source: <where the main and secondary light come from>
Direction: <front, side or backlight, and from which side>
Quality: <hard or soft, and where>
Temperature: <warm, cool or neutral, and where each one appears>
Contrast: <high or low; high-key or low-key>
Palette: <3 or 4 dominant colors>
Atmosphere: <fog, steam, dust, rain or clear air>
Lighting phrase for the prompt (English)
Lighting: <fonte> from the <lado>, <soft ou hard> light, <warm ou cool> tones, <low-key ou high-key>, <atmosfera, ex.: steam visible in the light beam>. Color palette: <cores>.

07 · To keep

Source and sideEvery light has a believable source and comes from one side. Say both.
Shadow countsTaking light away creates depth. Hard means tension; soft means warmth.
Fixed lookPalette and atmosphere on the look card, the same in every prompt of the scene.
